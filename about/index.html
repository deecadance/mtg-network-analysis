About the Method

Archetypes first. Then core cards, bombs, synergy and picks.

This site turns 17Lands Premier Draft data into one page per learned archetype. It first learns the archetypes from deck lists. Then, inside each archetype, it asks four questions: which cards the deck is built from, which cards win on their own, which pairs win more together than their separate effects predict, and which picks raise draft win rate.

The sections below describe the current implementation (analysis version 3).

1. Data

All statistics come from two 17Lands Premier Draft exports: the game export (one row per game, with the deck list and the cards in the opening hand and drawn) and the draft export (one row per pick). A card is in hand in a game when it was in the opening hand or drawn. Every “win rate in hand” on the site counts those games.

2. Archetypes from full deck lists

Let \(X\) be the deck-by-card matrix built from the deck_* columns of the game export. Each row is one deck build and each column is one card. Cards in more than 55% of decks, or in fewer than 250 decks, are removed before clustering: a card that almost every deck plays says little about which archetype a deck belongs to, and very rare cards add noise.

The remaining counts are weighted with TF-IDF (term frequency - inverse document frequency). Here the “term” is a card and the “document” is a deck. The first factor rewards cards that a deck plays; the second down-weights cards that many decks play.

\[ w(d,c) = \bigl(1 + \ln x(d,c)\bigr)\left(\ln\frac{1 + D}{1 + df(c)} + 1\right) \]
  • \(d\): one deck build; \(c\): one card.
  • \(x(d,c)\): copies of card \(c\) in deck \(d\).
  • \(D\): number of deck builds; \(df(c)\): number of deck builds that contain \(c\).

Each weighted deck row is scaled to unit length and reduced with truncated SVD (at most 48 components). The reduced rows are scaled to unit length again, so k-means compares decks by the direction of their composition (cosine geometry) rather than by deck size. Clustering is plain k-means with fixed seeds, so a rerun gives the same archetypes.

How many archetypes a set gets is decided by rules, not by a fixed number:

  • Colour-seeded (most sets). One cluster is seeded for every 17Lands main-colour group (a colour pair, or a three-colour clan in sets built around them) that covers at least 3% of decks. A colour group that no cluster clearly owns is dropped. So is a group whose cluster nearly duplicates another one (centroid cosine above 0.85).
  • Decks that cut across colours. Up to three extra clusters (for example Tarkir's five-colour Dragons, Lorwyn's Faeries or the TMNT Pizza deck) are added one at a time. Each is kept only if every cluster still holds at least 4% of decks, no two clusters are near-duplicates, and every cluster is stable: refitting on four 80% subsamples must find it again, with an average best-match Jaccard overlap of at least 0.75.
  • Colour-free search. Where colours do not organise the decks (Powered Cube), the number of clusters starts at 7 and grows, up to 11, while every cluster passes the same size, redundancy and stability checks; the last number that passes is used. Silhouette scores are recorded but do not choose the number.
  • Exports without colour columns (Strixhaven: School of Mages) get each deck's main colours from its contents: a colour counts when the deck has at least 4 nonland cards of it.

3. Signposts

For archetype \(k\), the signpost statistic asks whether a card appears much more often inside that archetype than outside it. Signposts name the archetype (its two title cards) and supply part of the cards shown.

\[ \ell(c) = \ln\frac{a_c + \alpha_c}{N_k - a_c + \beta_c} - \ln\frac{b_c + \alpha_c}{N_{\neg k} - b_c + \beta_c} \]
  • \(a_c\), \(b_c\): decks inside and outside archetype \(k\) that contain card \(c\).
  • \(N_k\), \(N_{\neg k}\): decks inside and outside archetype \(k\).
  • \(\ell(c)\): smoothed log-odds contrast. A positive value means the card is concentrated in the archetype.

The prior is centred on the card's prevalence across the whole format, which keeps the statistic stable for small samples. A rarity weight \(w_c\) slightly lowers the prior for rares and mythics.

\[ p_{0,c} = \frac{a_c + b_c}{D} \]
\[ \alpha_c = 1 + 28\,p_{0,c}\,w_c, \qquad \beta_c = 1 + 28\,(1-p_{0,c}) \]
\[ z(c) = \frac{\ell(c)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(\ell(c))}}, \qquad signpost(c) = z(c)\sqrt{a_c} \]

4. The cards shown

Each archetype page shows up to 32 cards, chosen in this order (lands are skipped):

  1. staples: up to 10 cards played in at least 25% of the archetype's decks;
  2. the 16 strongest signposts;
  3. up to 4 bomb slots: this archetype's bombs (section 9), strongest first, kept even when few decks play them;
  4. cards with the highest win rate in hand, then cards often played with the leading signposts;
  5. the most-played remaining cards.

Apart from the bomb slots, a card must be played in at least 5% of the archetype's decks. Every card shown needs at least 110 games in hand in the archetype.

5. Played together

The grey “played together” lines show which cards end up in the same finished decks more often than independent inclusion would predict.

\[ E(a,b) = N_k\,\pi(a)\,\pi(b), \qquad L_{ab} = \log_2\frac{O(a,b)+1}{E(a,b)+1} \]
  • \(\pi(a)\): share of the archetype's decks that contain card \(a\).
  • \(O(a,b)\): decks of the archetype that contain both cards; \(E(a,b)\): the count expected if the two were included independently.

A pair qualifies when at least 50 decks play both cards and \(L_{ab} > 0\). Its strength is \(L_{ab}\sqrt{O(a,b)}\), and each card keeps its 3 strongest pairs. These lines are a drafting signal (the cards are drafted into the same decks), not a claim that they win more together.

6. Synergy: verified pairs

For every pair of an archetype's cards, each game of the archetype falls in one cell of a 2×2 table (both cards in hand, only \(a\), only \(b\), neither) and in one stratum: game-length quintile × on the play or on the draw. The strata matter because card effects change with game length; without them, simulated data with no synergy at all produce false links.

\[ \operatorname{logit} P(\text{win}) = \alpha_s + \beta_{a,s}\,h_a + \beta_{b,s}\,h_b + \gamma\,h_a h_b \]
  • logit: log-odds of winning.
  • \(h_a\), \(h_b\): 1 when card \(a\) (or \(b\)) is in hand, else 0.
  • \(\alpha_s\), \(\beta_{a,s}\), \(\beta_{b,s}\): baseline and each card's own effect, free to differ in every stratum \(s\).
  • \(\gamma\): the interaction. \(\gamma > 0\) means the pair wins more often together than the two cards' separate effects, in the same kind of game, predict. The card spotlight shows it as a change in the odds of winning, \((e^{\gamma} - 1)\times 100\%\) (for example “+17% odds”).

\(\gamma\) and its standard error come from maximum likelihood. A pair is tested only when at least 90 games have both cards in hand and every other cell has at least 30 games; a stratum where one of the four cells has fewer than 5 games is left out of that pair's fit.

All tested pairs of an archetype then go through one Benjamini-Hochberg correction, which controls the false discovery rate: the expected share of false links among the links reported. Each pair gets a q-value.

  • Verified synergy: \(q \le 0.10\) and \(\gamma > 0.05\). These are the solid links in the Synergy view.
  • Tentative: \(q \le 0.25\) and \(\gamma > 0.05\), not verified. Drawn fainter and marked as tentative: treat them as leads.
  • Anti-synergy: \(q \le 0.10\) and \(\gamma < -0.05\).

A calibration check re-runs the whole gate on shuffled outcomes and on outcomes simulated from a model with no interactions. It fails if those produce more false links than the larger of 2 and 10% of the real ones.

What a verified synergy does not mean:

  • It mixes in-game and deck-level effects: a deck that runs both cards may simply be a better deck (a better drafter, a better curve).
  • Pairwise tables do not adjust for third cards: two cards that share a strong partner can look linked.
  • True interactions are small, so verified links are sparse and some archetypes have none. Their cards mostly win on their own.

7. Arrows: payoff and engine

The interaction \(\gamma\) has no direction. The arrows compare the two cards inside the pair's own games, using the games where only one of them was in hand:

\[ d_{ab} = \operatorname{logit} p_{a\,\text{only}} - \operatorname{logit} p_{b\,\text{only}} \]
\[ z_{ab} = \frac{d_{ab}}{SE(d_{ab})} \]
  • \(p_{a\,\text{only}}\): win rate of the archetype's games with \(a\) in hand and \(b\) not; \(p_{b\,\text{only}}\) likewise.

When \(|z_{ab}| \ge 1.96\), the arrow points from the card that wins more without its partner (the payoff) to the partner (the engine); otherwise no arrow is drawn. This describes standalone strength within the pair, not which card causes the synergy.

Payoff and engine rings are decided by synergy arrows only (verified and tentative). A card with at least two synergy arrows, at least 70% of them pointing out of it, is ringed as a payoff; at least 70% pointing into it, as an engine. Other cards get no ring. Played-together lines carry faint arrows for context, but they do not decide roles.

8. Core, support, periphery

The Core view lays each archetype out as an onion: core cards in the centre, support and periphery cards further out. Position follows coreness, which answers “how central is this card to the deck?”

\[ coreness(c) = M(c)\,\bigl(0.5 + 0.5\,G(c)\bigr) \]
\[ M(c) = \sqrt{\min\!\left(1, \frac{P_c}{0.60}\right)\cdot \left(\frac{\ln lift_c}{\ln 4}\right)_{[0,1]}} \]
\[ G(c) = 1 - \exp\!\left(-\left(\frac{S^{co}_c}{ref_{co}} + 0.5\,\frac{S^{syn}_c}{0.10}\right)\right) \]
  • \(M(c)\), membership: \(P_c\) is the share of the archetype's decks that play the card, \(lift_c\) its play rate here divided by its play rate in all decks. A card must be both widely played here and characteristic of here; below 5% play, \(M = 0\).
  • \(G(c)\), connectivity: \(S^{co}_c\) counts the card's played-together lines (\(ref_{co}\) is the archetype's median count), and \(S^{syn}_c\) sums the shrunk strength of its synergies (tentative ones at half weight). A card with no links keeps only half its membership, so it cannot reach the core.

Rings: core needs coreness at least 0.55 (the core holds at most 30% of the cards), support at least 0.30, and the rest is periphery. If fewer than 3 cards reach the core, the support cards with the highest coreness that have at least one link are promoted so the centre is never empty.

Separately from the rings, a card played in at least 15% of this archetype's decks, and at that rate in at most one other archetype of the set, is tagged signature. A card played in at least 25% of decks here and in at least 15% of decks in three or more archetypes is tagged staple. Other cards get no tag.

9. Bombs: strong on their own

Bombs answer a different question: does drawing this card win games on its own in this archetype? The estimate compares, within the decks that play the card, games where it was in hand with games where it stayed in the library, so the deck's own strength cancels out. It is taken relative to the archetype's typical card (the precision-weighted average of all card effects there).

Most cards are played in few games of any one archetype, so the estimate is shrunk with a two-level empirical-Bayes prior: first toward the card's effect in the set's other archetypes, then toward the set-wide spread of card effects. A card whose effect clearly depends on the archetype keeps its own estimate.

  • A card is a bomb when the shrunk estimate adds at least 5 win-rate points over a typical card of the deck, with a probability of at least 90% that it is better than typical. It needs at least 200 games in hand in decks that play it, and at least 50 where it was not seen.
  • The prior borrows strength from other archetypes, so three checks keep a card that this archetype barely plays from becoming one of its bombs: the archetype's own estimate must be positive with probability at least 75%, at least 1% of its decks must play the card, and the card may add at most one colour to the archetype's colours.
  • Bombs that are core or support cards keep their ring (with a star); the others sit in the separate island “Bombs - strong on their own” beside the rings.

10. Picks

The Picks view sizes cards by pick score and fades cards that do not raise draft win rate. Draft statistics come from the draft export: each draft is assigned to the archetype its picks match best.

Pick score (0-10)
\[ pick_{raw}(c) = 100\,W(c)\,\sqrt{\max(1,n_{draft}(c))}\;B(c) \]
\[ W(c) = \max(0,\Delta_{draft}(c)) + 0.55\max(0,\Delta_{hand}(c)) \]
\[ B(c) = 1 + 0.10\max(0, lift_c-1) \]
  • \(\Delta_{draft}(c)\): match win rate of the archetype's drafts that took the card, smoothed with 14 prior matches, minus the archetype's draft win rate.
  • \(\Delta_{hand}(c)\): win rate in hand minus the archetype's win rate.
  • \(n_{draft}(c)\): the archetype's drafts that took the card; \(lift_c\): signpost lift.

The raw value is transformed with \(\ln(1+x)\), clipped between the 5th and 95th percentiles of the archetype's cards, and rescaled to 0-10.

ALSA, wheel chance and pick urgency

ALSA (average last seen at) is the average pick at which the card was last seen in a pack, over the whole draft table. Wheel chance is the share of copies a drafter saw and passed that were still in the pack when it came back eight picks later.

\[ \begin{aligned} market(c) = 10\,\bigl(&0.48\,A_c^{0.58} + 0.22\,T_c^{0.72} \\ &+ 0.30\,(1 - wheel_c)\bigr) \end{aligned} \]
\[ u(c) = 0.084\,market(c) + 0.016\,pick(c) \]
\[ urgency(c) = 10\left(\frac{u(c) - 0.45}{0.55}\right)_{[0,1]} \]
  • \(A_c = \left(\tfrac{11 - ALSA(c)}{8}\right)_{[0,1]}\) and \(T_c = \left(\tfrac{11 - ATA(c)}{8}\right)_{[0,1]}\), where ATA is the average pick at which the card was taken; \((\cdot)_{[0,1]}\) clamps to the interval from 0 to 1.
  • \(market(c)\): how early the whole table takes the card, 0-10; \(pick(c)\): the pick score above.

Pick urgency mostly reflects how early the table takes the card; 10 means take it now. Cards that the table takes very early and that also raise win rate in the deck are set to 10.

Shell dependence (0-10)

Shown in the card spotlight. It is high when the card wins clearly more when drawn together with other cards of the deck than when drawn alone, and it is scaled down for cards that are already strong on their own.

11. Splash guide

Each set has a splash guide. It compares decks that splashed one off colour with decks of the same main colours that did not splash. The deck build is the sampling unit, so standard errors account for several games played with one deck. Each cell is shrunk toward the format-average splash effect (empirical Bayes) and labelled with a descriptive cost tier: gain, no measurable cost, small cost or large cost. Lands and splashed cards are a drill-down, not the evidence. When colours are inferred from deck contents (Strixhaven: School of Mages), the tiers are not shown.

These are observational comparisons. Drafters choose to splash for a reason, such as a bomb or a thin pool, so a splash cost is a description of what happened, not advice.
